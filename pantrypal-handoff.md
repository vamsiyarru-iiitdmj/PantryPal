## Decisions made

- Use the translated fields only. Rows containing Devanagari in a translated field are deferred to the planned Hindi-language upgrade, not silently mixed into the English catalog.
- Parse ingredient strings with parentheses-aware splitting and preserve raw text, quantity, normalized unit, aliases, preparation note, and optionality.
- Keep parenthetical aliases as evidence. Do not automatically merge ambiguous alias chains; `ingredient_alias_conflicts.csv` records the conflicts for review.
- Ingredient types are conservative, explainable seed metadata. `ingredient_types_needing_review.csv` is ordered by frequency; review it before enforcing category-specific unit validation in the app.

### Issues still open / known limitations

1. Quantity-only fragments are now mapped to `Unparsed ingredient fragment`, retaining raw text in `recipe_ingredients.csv`. The 44 placeholder rows are excluded from the compact tuple and reverse-lookup exports. They still require review and are not valid ingredient identities.
2. The alias-conflict report contains legitimate ambiguities as well as possible errors (for example, a transliterated alias may refer to both coriander seeds and coriander powder). Do not automatically resolve every conflict to one ingredient.
3. `ingredient_types_needing_review.csv` still contains many unclassified candidates; review the most frequent names first. Category and unit suggestions remain inferred, not web-verified or authoritative.
4. `quantity_raw` is text. The data is not yet safe for automatic inventory arithmetic or cooking-log stock deduction until quantity parsing, ingredient-specific unit conversion, and FEFO batch consumption are implemented and tested.
5. `recipe_ingredient_tuples.csv` overlaps with `recipe_ingredients.csv`, but is retained because the compact tuple export was explicitly requested. `ingredient_type_units.csv` is a category-level audit summary, not a per-ingredient mapping. The README marks review files as non-runtime exports.
